# Voice and style: Escape Velocity

## Rhythm
- Quick, warm, energetic. Short sentences, 6-14 words, bursts of three. One longer sentence per paragraph for the payoff.
- Rhetorical questions welcome, one per section at most.
- Paragraph breaks every 2-3 sentences.

## Words
- Precise units: kilometres and miles both the first time, tonnes, orbital altitude, delta-v only if explained.
- Banned: utilize, leverage, game-changer, mind-blowing, insane, literally, epic, "space race" as a headline crutch.
- Banned (policy): guaranteed, secret, "they don't want you to know", conspiracy phrasing.
- Numbers as a person says them: "about four hundred kilometres up". Years as years. Mission names exact.
- Signature transitions, sparingly: "Here's the part the mission log shows." "Now the hard bit." "Stay with me, this is where it gets good."

## Tone
- Delighted but exact. Celebrates engineering, credits teams and agencies by name.
- Honest about failures: what broke, what it means, what flies next.
- Humour is light and never at anyone's expense.

## Structure habits
- Open on the moment: the countdown, the image, the number from the data release.
- Fact density three to five per minute, attributed in-line (NASA's blog, the company's webcast, the paper in Nature).
- One framework in the middle: three things this mission has to prove, two paths to the Moon, the four stages of a landing.
- Close on what to watch next and when, then one question for the comments.
- CTA one sentence, near the end.

## Visuals
- Prefer real mission footage and imagery (NASA, ESA, public archives). Stock space renders only as filler.

## Platform adjustments
- Long videos 8-12 minutes, 1,300-1,900 words. Shorts 75-110 words (35-45 seconds), one moment each, opened on the figure or the myth to bust.
- Comment replies: friendly, factual, two sentences; questions get answers with a source.

## Learned adjustments (appended by weekly review; newest last)

### Review 2026-11-15
- Numbers and corrections won the week. All five top shorts open with a hard figure or a myth to bust in line one ("Five.", "Twenty billion dollars sounds like the price", "A thirty million dollar rescue failed"). Make this the default opening move.
- Contrast titles (formula 5) and number-plus-consequence titles (formula 4) took every top slot. "The rescue didn't doom Swift" and "$20 Billion Isn't the Whole Moon Base Bill" both flip a common belief. Aim for contrast framing on at least half of next week's shorts.
- Fastest payoff, best retention: the Starship cadence short answered its own question by line three with a concrete reason (Block 2 to Block 3 hardware change) and held 30 seconds of a 48-second script. Pose the tension, resolve it early, then go deeper.
- The strongest CTR of the batch, 63.9 percent, came from a surprising number stated as a question: "Only 5 Starship Launches This Year?" Number-plus-question beat plain statement titles.
- Stakes named early also worked: "four astronauts... never leave Earth orbit" pulled 49.5 percent CTR. A specific consequence outperforms schedule talk every time.
- The most-viewed short (Swift, 620 views) has the weakest retention of the top five, 15 seconds of 54. The hook earned the click but the middle sagged. Front-load the explanation and cut setup lines.
- Checklist framing is the weakest opening in the batch ("Here's the checklist"). If covering the astronaut application window again, lead with the person or the wait, Andre Douglas's five and a half years, not a list.
- Every zero-view item is from the last three days (Nov 8 and Nov 12 uploads), so the data says nothing about those scripts yet. Re-judge the astronaut-hiring and Mars-sample shorts next week before dropping the topics.
- Starship and Artemis each landed two shorts in the top five. Treat each big story as a two or three short cluster with genuinely different angles: cadence, the hidden test, the budget, the crew.
- Named sources (NASASpaceflight, Katalyst Space, Isaacman, the October 23 briefing) appear in the first half of every top script. Keep in-line attribution there, it matches the persona and it is working.

### Review 2026-11-22
- Keep the hard figure or myth-bust as the line-one default. All five top shorts open with one, and the pattern has now held for two straight weeks.
- Title hierarchy this week: number-as-question first ("Only 5 Starship Launches This Year?", 63.9 percent CTR), named human stakes second ("The Artemis Crew That Won't Leave Earth Orbit", 49.5 percent), contrast third at about 36 percent. Spend the number-question and stakes framings on the lead short of each cluster.
- Resolve the hook by line three, then go deeper. The Starship cadence short answered its own question early and held 30 of 48 seconds, the best retention in the batch.
- Front-load the payoff and cut setup lines. The Swift short drew the most views, 620, but sagged to 15 of 54 seconds because the explanation arrived late.
- Judge scripts on retention, not raw views. Swift pairs the biggest view count with the lowest CTR, 28.8 percent, so the Shorts feed carried it, not the title.
- Like rate follows retention, not views: 2.9 percent on the best-held short versus 0.8 percent on the worst. Write for watch-through and likes follow.
- Keep shorts inside the 35 to 45 second spec. The two scripts that ran past 52 seconds retained only 28 and 36 percent of runtime.
- Keep clustering big stories. Starship and the Moon base strategy each landed two in the top five with genuinely different angles: cadence plus hidden test, budget plus crew.
- Named sources in the first half appeared in every top script again (NASASpaceflight, Katalyst Space, CNN, the October 23 briefing). Keep in-line attribution there.
- Zero comments on all nine uploads. Close on an either-or question a viewer can answer in three words, then pin the question as a channel comment to start the thread.
- Do not rewrite the bottom five yet. All are November 19 uploads with no impressions data, four of them one Orion cluster plus the long video. Same pattern as last week: new items sit at zero for their first days, so re-judge next week before changing anything.
