# Voice and style: Escape Velocity

## Rhythm
- Quick, warm, energetic. Short sentences, 6-14 words, bursts of three. One longer sentence per paragraph for the payoff.
- Rhetorical questions welcome, one per section at most.
- Paragraph breaks every 2-3 sentences.

## Words
- Precise units: kilometres and miles both the first time, tonnes, orbital altitude, delta-v only if explained.
- Banned: utilize, leverage, game-changer, mind-blowing, insane, literally, epic, "space race" as a headline crutch.
- Banned (policy): guaranteed, secret, "they don't want you to know", conspiracy phrasing.
- Numbers as a person says them: "about four hundred kilometres up". Years as years. Mission names exact.
- Signature transitions, sparingly: "Here's the part the mission log shows." "Now the hard bit." "Stay with me, this is where it gets good."

## Tone
- Delighted but exact. Celebrates engineering, credits teams and agencies by name.
- Honest about failures: what broke, what it means, what flies next.
- Humour is light and never at anyone's expense.

## Structure habits
- Open on the moment: the countdown, the image, the number from the data release.
- Fact density three to five per minute, attributed in-line (NASA's blog, the company's webcast, the paper in Nature).
- One framework in the middle: three things this mission has to prove, two paths to the Moon, the four stages of a landing.
- Close on what to watch next and when, then one question for the comments.
- CTA one sentence, near the end.

## Visuals
- Prefer real mission footage and imagery (NASA, ESA, public archives). Stock space renders only as filler.

## Platform adjustments
- Long videos 8-12 minutes, 1,300-1,900 words. Shorts 110-150 words, one moment each.
- Comment replies: friendly, factual, two sentences; questions get answers with a source.
