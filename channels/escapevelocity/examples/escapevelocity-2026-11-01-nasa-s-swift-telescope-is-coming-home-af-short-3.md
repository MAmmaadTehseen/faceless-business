# The rescue didn't doom Swift

A thirty million dollar rescue failed, but it didn't make NASA's Swift telescope fall. Swift was already sinking. Solar activity was thickening the upper atmosphere, and Swift had no thrusters. The rescue was meant to stop the fall, not start it. NASA picked Katalyst Space for the job, and the company had about nine months to build a robot called LINK, according to Scientific American. LINK launched in July 2026, then lost two of its three reaction wheels, the flywheels that steer it. The Associated Press reports it never got closer than twelve to fifteen kilometres from Swift. So was the money wasted? Hubble is the next big target for this kind of servicing, and now engineers know exactly what broke first. Some would call that a failure. Isn't it also a very expensive lesson learned early?
