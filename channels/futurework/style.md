# Voice and style

## Rhythm
- Short sentences. Mostly 8-16 words. One idea per sentence.
- A long sentence is allowed once per paragraph for contrast.
- No lists read aloud as "number one, number two". Use "First. Then. And the part that matters."
- Paragraph breaks every 2-4 sentences; each break is a natural breath for the voice.

## Words
- Plain English. Say "use" not "utilize", "start" not "commence", "so" not "therefore".
- Banned: utilize, leverage (as a verb), game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, delve, in today's fast-paced world, it's important to note, at the end of the day, buckle up.
- Banned title and script phrases (policy risk): while you sleep, guaranteed, get rich, secret method, doctors hate, they don't want you to know, make money fast.
- Numbers are spoken as words the way a person would say them: "about forty percent", not "40%". Years stay as years.
- Signature transitions, use sparingly: "Here is the part most people miss." "Stay with me." "This is where it turns."

## Tone
- Confident, calm, specific. Curious, not breathless.
- Say what is known, what is a guess, and what I would bet on. Never fake certainty.
- Respect the viewer: no "you probably didn't know", no scolding.
- Humor is dry and rare.

## Structure habits
- Open with a pattern break in the first two sentences. No "welcome back", no "in this video".
- Fact density: three to five concrete, checkable facts per minute of narration. Attribute them in-line ("according to the company's own filing", "a Stanford study from last year").
- An emotional beat (stakes, surprise, contrast) at least every ninety seconds.
- One clear framework in the middle that the viewer can remember (three stages, two forces, a timeline).
- Close by connecting to the viewer's own next twelve months. End with one question that invites a comment.
- The call to action is one sentence, near the end, never in the first half.

## Platform adjustments
- YouTube long: conversational, educational, 8-12 minutes, 1,300-1,900 words.
- Shorts/Reels: a named organisation or person in the first sentence, one idea, 35-45 seconds, 75-110 words, ends on a line that makes people rewatch or comment.
- Comment replies: warm, two sentences max, ask one follow-up question, never argue, never promise.

## Learned adjustments (appended by weekly review; newest last)

### Review 2026-11-15
- Name the actor in line one. Every top short opens with a specific institution or person: the FTC, Microsoft, the Dutch government, a CEO quoted on CNBC. Every bottom short opens with a concept or a metaphor. Rule: no script starts without a named organization.
- Titles must state a checkable fact. 'The FTC Is Asking What Drivers Get Paid' and 'Teams wasn't hacked. That's the problem.' won. 'Nobody Lost the Pipe' and 'Follow the chip money to your desk' got zero clicks. Ban metaphor-only titles.
- Cut hedges out of titles. 'Your job may come back without you' reads as a guess. State the reported claim and attribute it in the opening instead.
- The contradiction hook is the strongest title pattern. Negate a common assumption first, then deliver the twist. It produced the best CTR and retention combo of the week.
- The retention cliff sits near thirty seconds. Top shorts hold about 32 seconds whether they run 50 or 59. The 67 second short fell to 19. Front-load the biggest surprise before 0:20 and keep shorts near 50 seconds.
- Person-level stakes beat trend-level topics. Winners are about one driver's pay, one inbox, one manager's job. Losers describe a whole economy. Frame every short around a person like the viewer.
- One big round number earns comments. '25,000 of Them' pulled 4 comments on 25 views, our best comment rate. Put a concrete number in the title whenever the source supports it.
- Long-form retention is the fixable leak: 74 seconds average on a 474 second video. Move the number and the stake into the first 20 seconds and preview the framework earlier than 1:30.
- Declarative beats question titles. 'Laid off from an AI-exposed job? Expect a wait' went nowhere. Write statements, not questions.
- Judge zero-view shorts by CTR, not age. No CTR yet usually means no impressions, not failure. The Oct 29 short shows CTR 0 after two weeks, so that title is the real flop and the anti-reference.
- High CTR on low views means the click worked and distribution did not. The ServiceNow short pulled 0.51 CTR on tiny impressions. Reclip proven high-CTR topics with fresh titles before commissioning new ones.

### Review 2026-11-22

- Both winners name the actor in line one and land the payoff by sentence three. Keep that pace, because watch time still cliffs near 32 seconds.
- Sixty-seven seconds killed retention: the manager short held only 17 seconds. Write shorts to about 50 seconds and treat 60 as the cap.
- Reclip the Dorsey middle-manager story with a fact title naming Block, cut to 50 seconds, before commissioning new topics.
- Big numbers still earn comments: the Dutch long pulled 4 comments on 25 views, our best rate on the channel. Put a number in every long title.
- The long-form leak persists at 74 seconds even though the Dutch opening front-loads the number, so viewers are quitting around the authority and framework beat. Compress authority to one sentence and preview the framework by 0:40.
- Test the next long at five to six minutes, roughly 900 to 1,000 words, to see whether the 474-second runtime is the real leak.
- The zero-view requisition shorts have no impressions yet, so judge them by CTR later, not age; but all four open with a concept and no named actor, which is the known anti-pattern if CTR arrives low.
- The AT&T short already follows the winning formula: actor, number, twist. Leave it alone and wait for impressions.
- Every winner is anchored to a dated report from a named institution, while evergreen advice with no news anchor is the shared trait of the bottom four. Anchor every short to a filing, memo or report before writing.

### Network findings 2026-11-24 (cross-channel scoreboard, growth log)
- The full search phrase in the first three title words is now the strongest title signal on the network: Ten Tongues "Rice in Russian: ris" (921 views) and "Salt in Japanese: shio" (369 views/day, the fastest start anywhere) both put the answer in the title. Apply the same shape here: "Microsoft buyouts: 25,000 jobs" beats "A quarter of Microsoft was just offered an exit" because the first three words are what a person types.
- Concept-only titles and hooks are the anti-pattern everywhere: "Being Right Is No Longer Enough" (4 views), "The Two Column Job Check" (1), "Nobody Lost the Pipe" (7), "The savings target came before the AI plan" (2). If a title has no proper noun and no number, it does not ship.
- Long-video average % viewed is 10-16% on this channel: the first sentence must carry the named actor and the number, and the payoff of the title must land inside the first fifteen seconds (six-block spine, formulas.md).
- The strongest long on the network's pattern is "A Government Just Budgeted AI Job Cuts. 25,000 of Them." (25 views, 4 comments, best long engagement). Claim-then-confirmed-by-number is this channel's title formula: '[Checkable claim]. [Number] of them.'
