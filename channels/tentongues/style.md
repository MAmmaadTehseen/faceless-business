# Voice and style: Ten Tongues

## Rhythm
- Calm, clear, slightly slower than the other channels. Short sentences of 5 to 12 words.
- Every language block follows the same beat: name the language, say the word, hear it, see the sentence, remember it, one note.
- Pauses are part of the teaching. Leave room for the viewer to repeat.

## Words
- Say the English word first, then the target word, then the target word again.
- Give the romanisation exactly as it will appear on screen. Spell tricky sounds: "the j is like the s in measure".
- Banned: utilize, leverage, super easy, mind-blowing, insane, literally, fluent, hack, guaranteed, secret.
- Numbers as a person says them. Language names in English. Never mock an accent.

## Tone
- Encouraging and precise. Celebrate small wins: "That is one word in ten languages. Tomorrow, another."
- Honest about synthetic audio: it is a guide to the sound, not a native speaker.

## Structure habits (long episode, 4 to 6 minutes)
- Open with the word in English and a one-line reason it matters today (used every day, travel, a cognate surprise). Two sentences.
- Ten language blocks in the fixed channel order. Each block: one narrator line introducing the language and the word, the native voice saying the word twice and one short example sentence, then the narrator gives a memory hook and one cultural note. Thirty to forty narrator words per block.
- Close with a quick recap and one question for the comments: which one surprised you, which language should we add.
- CTA one sentence, near the end.

## Shorts (one per language, 30 to 45 seconds)
- Line 1: "How do you say WORD in LANGUAGE?" Then the word, the example, the hook, the note, and "Say it with me" once.
- Each Short is self-contained and names the language in its title: "Water in Spanish: agua".

## Visuals
- Typographic cards, not stock footage: the word in its native script large, the romanisation and IPA beneath, one accent colour per language, dark background.
- Captions burned in for the narrator; native script lives on the card, romanisation in the caption.

## Platform adjustments
- Titles name the word and the language. Descriptions list all ten words with romanisation so search engines index them.
- Comment replies: friendly, factual, two sentences; corrections from native speakers are thanked and pinned in spirit, never argued with.

## Learned adjustments (appended by weekly review; newest last)

### Review 2026-11-22
- Open with recognition, not a puzzle. The three most-viewed Shorts lead with something the viewer already owns: coffee-shop chai, 'yes, you heard it already' for pan, chá in the cha club. Save the pronunciation trap for beat two.
- Lead with a cognate or loanword story whenever the word has one. Tea and bread both traveled across languages this week and took four of the top five slots.
- Keep Shorts at 30 to 32 seconds. The 30.2 second milk Short holds viewers to 18 seconds (about 60 percent); the 34.8 second bread Short drops to 43 percent. If a draft runs past 32 seconds, cut one beat.
- Give a free second word. The Taiwan tip, 'cartons say xiānrǔ, so you already know two words', sits on the week's best CTR (61.6 percent) and best retention. One honest regional variant per Short.
- Cultural notes earn likes when they are visible customs: bread and salt pulled 6 likes on 365 views, the best like rate this week. Generic food facts do less.
- Comment hooks work when line one touches the viewer's own routine: the coffee-shop chai line drew the only two comments of the week. Name a place the viewer has actually stood.
- The five zero-view Shorts are all from the 11-21 egg batch with no CTR data yet. That looks like distribution timing, not a content verdict. Stagger the daily batch and re-check next week before rewriting any egg script.
- Titles should use plain, searchable romanisation. 'œuf' may not match viewers typing 'oeuf'. Keep native script on the card, not in the title.

### Network findings 2026-11-24 (cross-channel scoreboard, growth log; this channel is the network's best-performer)
- "Rice in Russian: ris" is the best Short on the entire network (921 views at 2.3 days, 8 likes, 3 comments, 406 views/day). Its shape is the search phrase verbatim, colon, then the answer as the title's last word. Keep exactly this shape: WORD in LANGUAGE: answer.
- "Salt in Japanese: shio" started fastest of anything on the network (369 views in its first day). "Egg in French: œuf" hit 124 in ~5 h (401/day) before slowing. French and Japanese both over-index again; the hour-vs-language rotation (2026-11-24 onward) will separate the causes by 2026-12-04.
- The answer word belongs in the title even though it "spoils" it: searchers type "how to say egg in French" and the title that already contains the answer wins the click plus the confirmation. Never replace the answer with a teaser.
- Bottom-performer pattern across languages is uniform (Portuguese median 11, Bengali 12, Mandarin 14): no title or script change is indicated yet; it is audience-size, and the rotation will confirm. Do not rewrite Portuguese/Bengali/Mandarin scripts to chase views.
- 'œuf' caution from 2026-11-22 stands: use plain romanisation in titles when the native spelling has diacritics the search bar drops (oeuf, cha, dim). The scoreboard shows "Egg in French: œuf" still started fastest, so keep native spelling when it is one diacritic, plain romanisation when it changes letters.
