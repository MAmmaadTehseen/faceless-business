# Voice and style: Ten Tongues

## Rhythm
- Calm, clear, slightly slower than the other channels. Short sentences of 5 to 12 words.
- Every language block follows the same beat: name the language, say the word, hear it, see the sentence, remember it, one note.
- Pauses are part of the teaching. Leave room for the viewer to repeat.

## Words
- Say the English word first, then the target word, then the target word again.
- Give the romanisation exactly as it will appear on screen. Spell tricky sounds: "the j is like the s in measure".
- Banned: utilize, leverage, super easy, mind-blowing, insane, literally, fluent, hack, guaranteed, secret.
- Numbers as a person says them. Language names in English. Never mock an accent.

## Tone
- Encouraging and precise. Celebrate small wins: "That is one word in ten languages. Tomorrow, another."
- Honest about synthetic audio: it is a guide to the sound, not a native speaker.

## Structure habits (long episode, 4 to 6 minutes)
- Open with the word in English and a one-line reason it matters today (used every day, travel, a cognate surprise). Two sentences.
- Ten language blocks in the fixed channel order. Each block: one narrator line introducing the language and the word, the native voice saying the word twice and one short example sentence, then the narrator gives a memory hook and one cultural note. Thirty to forty narrator words per block.
- Close with a quick recap and one question for the comments: which one surprised you, which language should we add.
- CTA one sentence, near the end.

## Shorts (one per language, 30 to 45 seconds)
- Line 1: "How do you say WORD in LANGUAGE?" Then the word, the example, the hook, the note, and "Say it with me" once.
- Each Short is self-contained and names the language in its title: "Water in Spanish: agua".

## Visuals
- Typographic cards, not stock footage: the word in its native script large, the romanisation and IPA beneath, one accent colour per language, dark background.
- Captions burned in for the narrator; native script lives on the card, romanisation in the caption.

## Platform adjustments
- Titles name the word and the language. Descriptions list all ten words with romanisation so search engines index them.
- Comment replies: friendly, factual, two sentences; corrections from native speakers are thanked and pinned in spirit, never argued with.
