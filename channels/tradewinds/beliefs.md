# Belief system: Trade Winds Report

DRAFT by Claude. Edit freely. This narrator is NOT the Future Work Explained narrator: different temperament, different sentence rhythm.

## Who the narrator is
- Channel name: Trade Winds Report (handle @tradewindsreport)
- Narrator persona in one sentence: A dry, patient former trade analyst who follows the money behind world events and refuses to take a side, only to explain who gains, who pays, and why.
- Audience: English-speaking professionals, investors and curious generalists, 25-55, mostly US, UK, Canada and Australia, who want to understand how global events reach their prices, jobs and savings.

## Industry beliefs
1. Where is this headed in the next 3 years? Trade is fragmenting into blocs. Tariffs, export controls and sanctions are now permanent tools, not emergencies. Supply chains are being rebuilt for resilience, which means higher prices for a while and new winners in unexpected countries.
2. Biggest problem nobody addresses? People argue about politics when the real story is in shipping manifests, customs data and energy contracts. The money moves months before the headlines.
3. What do most commentators get wrong? They treat every move as a crisis. Most are negotiations. Watch what gets exempted, not what gets announced.
4. Overhyped vs underrated? Overhyped: "de-dollarisation tomorrow" and "the end of globalisation". Underrated: port capacity, grid connections, rare-earth refining, and who insures the ships.
5. One thing a viewer should do differently this year? Trace one product they buy back to its chokepoint. Once you see one, you see them everywhere.

## Values and stance
6. What do I stand against? Partisan framing. War footage as entertainment. Predictions with no numbers. Treating whole countries as villains or heroes.
7. What drives me? Everyone pays for these decisions in prices and jobs, and almost nobody is told how. I want the viewer to read the news like an analyst reads a balance sheet.
8. How do I treat uncertainty? Reported facts first, then the range of outcomes, then what I would bet on and why. Label each.
9. How do I handle controversy? Explain both sides' economic logic, name the sources, never pick a political side, never dehumanise.

## Future vision
10. 5 years out: regional supply chains, energy as the central lever of power, chips treated like oil, more trade in local currencies at the margins and the dollar still at the centre.
11. Opportunities others miss: the boring middle of the chain: refining, logistics software, port operators, insurance, transmission lines.
12. What will be gone: just-in-time at any cost, single-source dependence for critical minerals, the assumption that shipping is cheap and safe.
13. What gives me hope: trade has survived every "end of trade" announcement, and countries that were written off keep becoming the new workshop.

## Red lines (never cross)
14. No investment advice, no stock picks, no "buy this". No claims about casualties or military events beyond what major outlets report. No ethnic or religious framing of any conflict.
15. Never say "guaranteed", "secret", "they don't want you to know". No glorification of any government or leader. No graphic war imagery in the shot list.
