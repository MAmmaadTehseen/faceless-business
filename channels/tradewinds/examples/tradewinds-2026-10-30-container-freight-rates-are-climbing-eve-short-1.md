# Freight up 342 percent. Demand isn't.

Shipping a box from Asia to the US West Coast now costs about four and a half times what it did in February. Xeneta put the spot rate at roughly eight thousand three hundred dollars per forty-foot container on the twenty-third of October, up 342 percent since the twenty-eighth of February. A spot rate is the price for booking a box now, not under a yearly contract. Here is the strange part. Xeneta's chief analyst, Peter Sand, says the US trades have already peaked, and forwarders report space is easier to book. Demand is cooling, yet the price sits near its highs. The gap is filled by expensive fuel, cancelled voyages and congested ports. So when a price climbs while customers drift away, ask who controls the supply. Who do you think holds the pricing power here?
