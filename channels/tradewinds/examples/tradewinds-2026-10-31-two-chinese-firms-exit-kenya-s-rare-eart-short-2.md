# Who pays when the rare earth price floor bites

Washington promised to pay one hundred and ten dollars for something the market was selling for ninety-five. In July 2025, the US Department of Defense gave MP Materials a ten-year price floor of one hundred and ten dollars per kilogram for NdPr oxide, the key ingredient in magnets. In March 2026, it restructured a deal with Australia's Lynas around the same floor. Then, in August, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence reported the price outside China had slipped to about ninety-five dollars a kilogram. The producers are protected. Magnet buyers get cheaper material. And the gap between the two numbers lands on a public budget, which means taxpayers. Supporters call that insurance against supply shocks. Others see a subsidy for output the market is not yet absorbing. Which side of that ledger would you sit on?
