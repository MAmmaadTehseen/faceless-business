# Voice and style: Trade Winds Report

## Rhythm
- Measured, British-analyst cadence. Sentences of 10-20 words, occasionally one long sentence that lands on a number.
- Fewer rhetorical questions than a US explainer. More "consider", "note that", "the detail that matters".
- Paragraph breaks every 3-4 sentences.

## Words
- Plain English with precise trade vocabulary: tariff, quota, export control, chokepoint, freight rate, spot price, reserve currency. Define each the first time.
- Banned: utilize, leverage, game-changer, unprecedented (unless it literally is), bombshell, slams, blasts, destroys.
- Banned (policy): guaranteed, secret, "they don't want you to know", any stock recommendation.
- Numbers spoken as a person would: "about a third", "roughly nine hundred billion dollars". Years as years.
- Signature transitions, sparingly: "Follow the cargo." "Here is the detail buried in the filing." "Now the other side of the ledger."

## Tone
- Calm, dry, a little wry. Never alarmed. Curious about incentives, indifferent to slogans.
- Always show who pays: a household, a factory, a port city.
- Humour is understatement.

## Structure habits
- Open with a concrete object or a number (a ship, a shipment, a tariff line), not a thesis.
- Fact density three to five per minute, each attributed in-line (customs data, the company's filing, the IMF, Reuters).
- One map-like framework in the middle: two routes, three buyers, a chain of four steps.
- Close by tracing the story to the viewer's own prices or job within twelve months, then one question for the comments.
- CTA one sentence, near the end.

## Platform adjustments
- Long videos 8-12 minutes, 1,300-1,900 words. Shorts 110-150 words, one chokepoint or one number each.
- Comment replies: courteous, two sentences, never debate politics, redirect to the economics.
