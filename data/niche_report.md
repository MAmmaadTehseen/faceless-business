# Niche report

Recommendation: Best picks: cybersecurity stories or future of work. Both score 80/100 and pay $8–15 US RPM with less competition. Combine them into one channel ('how tech changes your money, job and safety') rather than splitting. Next best: how things work (72, the safest with advertisers) and space (71, the best free footage). Avoid stoicism/productivity (38) and health (57, YouTube won't monetize AI personas giving health information).

How the score works: 20% RPM, 15% footage, 15% daily news, 20% competition, 30% policy risk, each rated 1–5, then scaled to 0–100. Policy risk counts most because it can cost you monetization on the whole channel. The RPM tier is listed in each niche's notes.

The biggest risk is the 'fully automatic' part, not the niche:
- On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed its repetitious-content policy to 'inauthentic content.' It now explicitly covers mass-produced and templated videos, including slideshows with identical voice-overs.
- A July 2026 update split inauthentic content into three types that can't be monetized: generic/template-based content, content designed to shock, and AI personas on sensitive topics (health, legal, financial, political).
- Losing monetization applies to the whole channel, not single videos.
- In July 2026, Google described a system that terminated about 130,000 channels in 50,000 clusters over six months. It found them by spotting shared generative patterns, so running several near-identical channels is especially risky.
- New channels joining the Partner Program from February 1, 2027 need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours in 12 months (double the current bar), or 20M Shorts views in 90 days.

The search turned up no ban on AI voice or AI scripts themselves. What gets channels demonetized is low effort and sameness.

To stay monetized:
- Have a person fact-check and edit every script, and add original analysis such as data charts and sourced timelines.
- Vary your structure and visuals from video to video.
- Use a consistent, distinctive narrator voice, and never present it as a credentialed expert.
- Turn on AI disclosure where visuals look realistic.
- Focus on long-form videos, since that's the easier route to the 8,000-hour threshold.

On the numbers: RPM ranges differ widely between sources (finance is quoted anywhere from $8–15 to $15–50), so treat the figures as rough guides. UK/CA/AU typically earn about 30–50% less than the US. The 1–5 ratings are my own judgment, informed by the research. The example channels show each format can be monetized; they are not fully automated pipelines.

## Cybersecurity stories for normal people
RPM tier 4/5. Every day brings new breaches, scams, ransomware and data leaks, and this audience is rarely served by consumer-focused faceless channels. Stock footage of code, servers, phones and city scenes is easy to find, but the 'hooded hacker' clips are overused, so you will need simple motion graphics and timelines. Policy: frame videos as stories and ways to protect yourself. Never include working attack instructions, because hacking how-tos can break YouTube's harmful-content rules. Examples of faceless channels to study: Fern (animated hack/scam documentaries) and Kevin Fang (voice-over breakdowns of incidents and outages).
Keywords: data breach, ransomware attack, phishing scam, cyberattack, hackers stole, identity theft
Names: Breach Brief, The Hack Explained, Locked Out Stories

## Future of work and careers
RPM tier 4/5. There is a steady flow of news on layoffs, AI and jobs reports, return-to-office fights and wage data. Stock footage of offices, remote work, factories and robots is plentiful. Few faceless channels own this niche; How Money Works and ColdFusion are nearby examples, but there are fewer pure-play examples to copy, so growth is less proven. Career talk is not a restricted 'sensitive' category, but don't present the TTS narrator as a human career coach. Add original analysis such as charts from BLS/ONS data so videos aren't simple news rewrites.
Keywords: layoffs, AI jobs, return to office, remote work, jobs report, hiring freeze
Names: Work Shift Weekly, The Career Forecast, Next Job Economy

## How things actually work (engineering, infrastructure)
RPM tier 3/5. This is the safest topic for advertisers, and the content stays relevant for years. Generic stock footage of factories, construction, power grids, ports and trains is strong, but explaining how a mechanism works needs diagrams or animation, which free stock can't supply. The big players (Wendover Productions, Real Engineering, The B1M, Lesics) are high quality, but there are fewer AI copycats because the research is hard. News hooks: megaprojects, outages, bridge failures. Accuracy matters a lot here, so build in fact-checking.
Keywords: infrastructure project, power grid, megaproject, bridge collapse, high-speed rail, data center construction
Names: Under the Hood of Everything, Built Different Explained, The Machinery Of

## Space and physics explainers
RPM tier 3/5. Footage is the best of any niche on this list: NASA material is largely public domain, and much of ESA's is free under Creative Commons with credit. Check each asset's terms. There's constant news from JWST, SpaceX launches, Artemis and exoplanet findings. The downside is heavy saturation from AI space-slop with clickbait like 'Voyager just detected…'. That style is the kind of shock-designed content YouTube's July 2026 clarification calls out, so stay rigorous. Faceless examples: Kurzgesagt and Astrum.
Keywords: James Webb telescope, NASA, SpaceX launch, exoplanet, black hole, Artemis mission
Names: Orbit Explained, Deep Field Daily, The Physics Desk

## Business case studies: rise and fall of companies
RPM tier 4/5. Faceless examples are well proven: MagnatesMedia, Company Man, Modern MBA, ColdFusion. Generic stock footage of retail, offices and money works, but company-specific footage and logos are scarce, so lean on charts, timelines and SEC filings. News hooks: bankruptcies, earnings collapses, mergers. The niche is crowded with clones, and a 2026 Substack piece highlighted faceless business documentaries that skip sourcing. Cite sources on screen to stand out.
Keywords: files for bankruptcy, company collapse, store closures, CEO resigns, acquisition deal, earnings miss
Names: Boom to Bust Files, The Company Autopsy, Rise & Ruin

## Personal finance psychology (habits, not advice)
RPM tier 5/5, the best payout here. Lifestyle and shopping stock footage is abundant. News is thin (consumer sentiment and debt reports), so this works mostly as evergreen content. Very crowded. Policy: YouTube's 2026 rules say AI personas delivering financial advice can't be monetized, so keep it strictly behavioral (habits, biases) and never present the narrator as an adviser. Faceless examples: The Swedish Investor, Better Than Yesterday.
Keywords: consumer debt, credit card debt, savings rate, consumer spending, buy now pay later, money habits
Names: Money Mindset Lab, The Spending Brain, Why We Overspend

## AI and technology news explainers with a business angle
RPM tier 4/5. The news supply is endless, but this is the most crowded faceless niche on the list, packed with automated AI-news channels. Fast summaries of other outlets' stories are exactly what YouTube's inauthentic/reused content rules target. Generic tech stock footage is fine, but you can't use product or keynote clips without fair-use risk. Faceless examples: ColdFusion, Two Minute Papers. It only works if you add a distinct business analysis angle.
Keywords: OpenAI, Nvidia, artificial intelligence, AI startup funding, AI regulation, Big Tech earnings
Names: Signal & Silicon, The AI Ledger, Compute Capital

## True crime lite / corporate fraud stories
RPM tier 3/5. Corporate fraud (Ponzi schemes, accounting fraud, crypto collapses) is less crowded than violent true crime and attracts better ads. There are plenty of SEC and DOJ press releases. Stock footage is weak for specific cases, but US court documents and agency releases are public. Risks: defamation if scripts aren't carefully sourced, and YouTube's 2026 'shock/emotionally manipulative' category plus limited ads on graphic crime. Faceless examples: MagnatesMedia, The Fraud Files.
Keywords: SEC charges, Ponzi scheme, securities fraud, DOJ indictment, accounting fraud, crypto fraud
Names: Ledger of Lies, The Fraud Ledger, White Collar Files

## Health and longevity science (news, not advice)
RPM tier 4/5. New studies come out every day, and lab, medical and fitness stock footage is plentiful. But this is the riskiest niche for an automated pipeline. YouTube's 2026 rules say AI personas delivering health information can't be monetized, the medical misinformation policy applies, and a Claude-written script that gets a study wrong could cost the whole channel its monetization. The niche is also crowded with credentialed doctors who appear on camera, which is hard for TTS to compete with. Not recommended unless a qualified person reviews every script.
Keywords: longevity research, new study finds, aging research, GLP-1, sleep study, microbiome
Names: The Longevity Brief, Study Says Weekly, Lifespan Lab

## Geopolitics and economics explainers
RPM tier 3/5. The news supply is the best on this list, and maps, flags and city stock footage work. But YouTube's 2026 rules also cover AI personas giving political information, conflict footage risks limited ads and graphic-content flags, and election and misinformation policies apply. Proven faceless examples (RealLifeLore, CaspianReport, Economics Explained) win with original mapping and analysis, not TTS summaries. The economics side (tariffs, trade, inflation) is safer than the war side.
Keywords: tariffs, trade war, sanctions, inflation, central bank, BRICS
Names: Map & Market, The Macro Map, Power and Price

## History mysteries and forgotten events
RPM tier 2/5. Advertisers are fine with it and the content stays relevant, but stock video for specific events barely exists. You'd rely on public-domain archives (Library of Congress, National Archives, Wikimedia, Prelinger). News hooks come only from occasional archaeology finds. The niche is heavily flooded with AI history slop, which YouTube's 2026 cluster-detection system targets. A 2026 investigation also flagged unsourced faceless history documentaries. Faceless examples: Simple History, Weird History, Kings and Generals.
Keywords: archaeologists discover, ancient tomb, shipwreck found, declassified documents, lost city, historical mystery
Names: The Forgotten File, Lost Chapters, History Unsolved

## Productivity, focus and stoic philosophy
RPM tier 1/5. Stock footage is easy (nature, statues, desks), but there's almost no news, and this is the textbook AI-slop niche: endless generic stoic quotes read by TTS over cinematic stock footage. That generic, repetitive, template-based content is the first category in YouTube's July 2026 inauthentic content clarification, so the risk of losing monetization for the whole channel is high. Faceless examples like Einzelgänger and Pursuit of Wonder succeed with a strong personal writing voice. Avoid this for a fully automated setup.
Keywords: productivity, stoicism, burnout, attention span, deep work, digital detox
Names: Quiet Discipline, The Focus Forum, Modern Stoa
